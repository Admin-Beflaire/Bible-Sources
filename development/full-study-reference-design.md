## Original layers and their purpose

The versioned Theology source has four explicit parts:

| Part | Function | Adaptation for a passage study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I: Confessional Statements | Ten concise theological statements and a summary. | State the passage's warranted central conclusions clearly. Do not invent ten statements for every passage. |
| II: Explanation and Biblical Foundation | Explains each statement, why it matters and its biblical basis; concludes with a summary. | Readable explanation showing how claims follow from the passage and biblical story. |
| III: Exegesis, Canonical Context, and Connections | Demonstrates the linguistic, historical and canonical argument behind each statement; concludes with a synthesis. | Detailed, verse-linked analysis and explained connections. |
| IV: Reference Material | Supports the preceding argument with lexical/textual resources and tables; ends with a closing word. | A useful reference appendix, consistent with the analysis and actual sources consulted. |

The current website main pages combine concise statement and readable explanation; exegesis and references remain linked companion pages. The original's summaries and closing word provide coherent conclusions, not additional unrelated doctrinal topics. Adapt this relationship among conclusion, explanation, evidence and reference support rather than mechanically copying its publication order before the plugin's initial translation.

## Exegetical dimensions

All three source exegesis pages have ten topics corresponding to their main study. Theology 1.4.2 repeats five numbered analysis labels in each of its ten entries and names a sixth, rendering reconciliation where applicable, in its introduction. The website Theology follows that pattern; Christology and Pneumatology make the sixth dimension an explicit heading in every entry. Pneumatology names the integration dimension for both Christ and the Spirit.

| Dimension | Required Full-study substance when relevant |
| --- | --- |
| Source-language semantics | Consequential forms, grammar, contextual senses, related terms and lexical distinctions. Use supported etymology where helpful; do not turn a root into doctrinal proof. |
| Historical-cultural context and comparison | Specific attested practices or ancient works illuminating the passage, including meaningful similarities and contrasts. |
| Narrative trajectory | The theme's development through the biblical story and relevant covenantal turning points: continuity, change and fulfillment. |
| Intertextual evidence | Particular quotations, probable echoes, thematic developments and typological proposals, with the wording/context supporting each relationship. |
| Christ/Spirit integration | How the established argument relates to Christ, the Spirit and God's purposes, preserving the passage's local meaning. |
| Rendering reconciliation | Meaningful translation alternatives and the grammatical/contextual reasons for the chosen wording, including what changes under another rendering. |

Narrative trajectory follows a theme's movement through Scripture. Intertextual analysis demonstrates a particular textual relationship. Theological synthesis explains their significance. These should not collapse into a list of cross-references or an application paragraph. Do not force a speculative connection merely to fill a heading.

The versioned Theology reference section specifically contains word studies, an explained key-text table, major translation/textual notes, a names/titles table, abbreviations, source texts and a closing word. Its reference layer supports the preceding argument rather than starting a new doctrinal argument.

Do not copy the originals' bibliography as evidence of new consultation. Their BHS/BHQ, Rahlfs-Hanhart, NA28/UBS5 or lexicon references do not establish plugin access to those works. Name actual retrieved WLC/OSHB, Brenton Greek, SBLGNT or other material correctly. Distinguish manuscript, modern edition, lexical source, project-curated record and secondary interpretation.

## Separate later dependency: manuscript variance

The originals' reconciliation of renderings often concerns different translations of the same wording, such as Spirit/wind or Advocate/Helper. That is distinct from manuscript variants. Existing grammatical/translation notes remain valid within consulted evidence; they do not constitute systematic aligned manuscript comparison.

The additional section, translation variance across manuscripts, remains blocked by the full verse-mapping index for supported sources. Proposed placement remains after primary translation/notes, before local explanation.
